How to Convert RTF to PDF Without Uploading It Anywhere

November 7, 2026 · 3 min read

Most "RTF to PDF" results you find are online converters, and they all work the same way: you hand your document to a stranger's server, wait for it to come back, and hope the deletion policy means what it says. For a recipe that is fine. For a contract, an invoice, a medical letter, or anything with a name on it, it is an avoidable risk — because PDF conversion can happen entirely on your own device. Here are three ways, from fastest to most familiar.

Method 1: in your browser, with a free extension

The RTF to PDF extension for Chrome converts the file inside your browser:

  1. Install the extension from the Chrome Web Store (free, no account).
  2. Click its icon in the toolbar and drop your .rtf file in.
  3. Press Convert — the PDF lands in your download folder.

Nothing is uploaded, which brings three practical benefits along with the privacy: it works offline, there is no file size cap from somebody's free tier, and there is no queue — you are not sharing somebody's server with the rest of the internet.

Method 2: Microsoft Word

If the file is already open in Word: File → Save As → PDF (or File → Export → Create PDF). This converts offline and keeps formatting, at the cost of owning Word and opening the app for what is a ten-second job.

Method 3: LibreOffice Writer (free)

LibreOffice opens RTF natively and exports to PDF the same way: File → Export As → Export as PDF. It is free on Windows, macOS, and Linux, and everything runs locally.

What actually happens with an online converter

To be fair to them: established online converters use HTTPS, publish retention policies, and reputable ones do delete files on schedule. But the mechanics are unavoidable — your document is transmitted to, stored on, and processed by someone else's computer. Even with the best policy in the world, the file existed outside your control for a window of time. Offline conversion simply has no such window.

Frequently asked questions

Are online RTF to PDF converters safe?

Reputable ones are honest about deleting files after a period, but the fact remains: your document is transmitted to and processed on somebody else's server. For contracts, invoices, medical or legal papers, a converter that never uploads the file is the safer category.

Is there an offline RTF to PDF converter?

Yes. The free RTF to PDF extension for Chrome converts entirely in your browser with no upload, and Word or LibreOffice can export an open RTF file to PDF from the Save As / Export menu.

Does converting RTF to PDF lose formatting?

With a converter built for the job, no: fonts, colors, tables and images are kept, and the text in the PDF stays selectable and copyable. Quality drops mainly when a converter re-processes the file on a server or rasterizes it to an image.

Is there a file size limit for offline conversion?

Not one imposed by a server, since nothing is sent anywhere. The only limit is your own device's memory, which comfortably handles documents far larger than any online converter's free tier allows.

Do offline converters work without internet?

Yes. The RTF to PDF extension converts offline after installation, and word processors obviously do. That is a direct consequence of the file never leaving your device.
