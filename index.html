RTF to PDF, in your browser.

A free Chrome extension. Nothing is uploaded: your files are converted on your own device, and the text in the PDF stays selectable and copyable.

Add to Chrome Free, from the Chrome Web Store.
  • No data collected
  • Works offline
  • No account

How it works

  1. Drop your files.

    Click the extension icon and drag one .rtf file, or twenty, into the window. Anything that is not .rtf is filtered out.

  2. Press Convert.

    Each file shows its own progress. Change your mind? Cancel the whole run with one click.

  3. Download.

    Take a single PDF, or all of them as one ZIP archive. Everything happened in your browser, at your desk.

What you get

A queue, not a queue-jump

Add more files at any time. Duplicates and non-RTF strays are filtered out before they can waste a second of converting.

Cancel at any moment

Every file shows its own progress, and one click stops the whole run. Nothing is half-written behind your back.

One PDF, or one ZIP

Download a single file as PDF, or the whole batch as one ZIP archive. The queue and finished PDFs survive closing the popup.

It lives in your toolbar.

Pin RTF to PDF once and the converter stays one click away on every page. The welcome page after install shows you how.

Light and dark

The extension follows your system theme, with a manual switch in the corner of its window, same as this site.

Your files never leave your device.

Conversion happens in your browser's memory. No uploads, no servers, no accounts, and it works offline.

Questions

How do I convert an RTF file to PDF?

Install the extension, click its icon in the Chrome toolbar, drop your file and press Convert.

Are my files uploaded anywhere?

No. Everything happens in your browser's memory, on your device. The extension works offline.

Does it need an internet connection?

Not after the install. Converting works offline, which is exactly why nothing can leak in transit.

Can I convert several files at once?

Yes. Queue as many .rtf files as you need and download them separately or as one ZIP archive.

Will the formatting stay the same?

Fonts, colors, tables and images are kept, and the text in the PDF stays selectable and copyable.

Can I convert .doc or .docx files?

No, the converter is built for .rtf. Save your document as RTF first (most word processors can), then convert it here.

Where does the result end up?

In your browser's download folder, like any normal download. Your original files are never modified.

Does it work on a Mac or Windows?

It works in Chrome and other Chromium-based browsers on any desktop system.

How do I pin the icon to the toolbar?

Click the puzzle piece in Chrome's toolbar and pin RTF to PDF; the page you see right after install walks you through it.

Is it really free?

Yes. No account, no watermarks, no file limits.

What is RTF?

Rich Text Format, a document format from the late eighties that most word processors still open. A PDF looks the same everywhere; an RTF does not. That is the whole point of converting.
